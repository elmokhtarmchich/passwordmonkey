Save Passwords to Your Browser

Last updated: October 2026

Your browser can save passwords for you. When you log in to a website, your browser may show a prompt asking whether to save the username and password. Click Save, and the browser fills them in for you the next time you visit. Saving passwords makes it easier to use a different strong password for every site, since you do not have to remember or type them each time.

Honest note: the prompt does not appear on every site. If it does not show up, you can add the entry yourself in the browser's password settings.

Entries are filed under the page's origin — for example, passwordmonkey.org — not under the name of the site the password is for.

Chrome

Chrome saves passwords to your Google Account when you are signed in to Chrome with sync turned on. Saved passwords sync across your signed-in devices, and you can view and manage them at passwords.google.com. If you use a work or school Chrome profile, your administrator can turn password saving off.

After you save a password, Chrome offers to fill your username and password when you return to the site. Chrome also checks saved passwords against known data breaches and warns you if one was exposed, so you can change it right away. Saved passwords also work on Android and iOS when you sign in to Chrome with the same Google Account. You can edit a saved password or delete a site's entry anytime in Chrome's password manager.

  1. Go to the site and log in.
  2. When Chrome offers to save the password, click Save.
  3. If the prompt does not appear: open chrome://password-manager/passwords, click Add, and enter the site, username, and password.
  4. If you chose "Never save" for a site, remove it from Declined sites and apps, then try again.

Edge

Edge saves passwords to your Microsoft Account when you are signed in with sync turned on. Edge now uses device authentication — Windows Hello on Windows and Touch ID on Mac — instead of a custom primary password.

After you save a password, Edge offers to fill your credentials on your next visit. You can view all saved passwords in Microsoft Password Manager, and Edge asks for your device authentication before showing a saved password. Saved passwords also sync to Edge on iPhone, iPad, and Android when you sign in with the same Microsoft Account. You can edit or remove saved passwords there at any time.

  1. Go to the site and log in.
  2. When Edge offers to save the password, click Save.
  3. If the prompt does not appear: go to Settings > Passwords and autofill > Microsoft Password Manager [VERIFY menu names].
  4. Edge uses device authentication (Windows Hello, Touch ID) instead of a custom primary password.

Firefox

Firefox syncs saved logins to your Firefox Account when you are signed in with sync turned on, so your saved passwords follow you to your other devices.

Firefox offers to fill saved logins when you return to a site. You can view and edit them at about:logins. Firefox can also lock saved logins behind a primary password that you set. Saved passwords also appear in Firefox for iPhone and Android when you sign in with the same Firefox Account. You can turn sync on or off in Firefox settings, and remove saved logins from about:logins at any time.

  1. Go to the site and log in.
  2. When Firefox offers to save the password, click Save.
  3. If the prompt does not appear: go to about:logins and create a new login [VERIFY menu names].

Safari / iCloud

Safari saves passwords to iCloud Keychain, which syncs them across your Apple devices signed in with the same Apple ID [VERIFY].

Safari offers AutoFill for passwords when you sign in to a site. You can see and manage saved passwords in the Passwords app or in Settings > Passwords [VERIFY menu names]. You need your device passcode or Face ID / Touch ID to view a saved password. On iPhone and iPad, saved passwords can also be filled into apps, not just websites.

  1. Go to the site and log in.
  2. When Safari offers to save the password, click Save Password.
  3. Manage saved passwords in the Passwords app or Settings > Passwords [VERIFY menu names].

Samsung Internet / Samsung Pass

Samsung Pass is part of Samsung Wallet and works with Samsung Internet [VERIFY]. It can use your fingerprint, face, or PIN to unlock saved passwords. Samsung Internet can also use Google autofill [VERIFY menu names].

  • Look for the save prompt when you log in to a site in Samsung Internet.
  • Saved passwords can sync through Samsung Pass / Samsung Wallet [VERIFY].

  • You can view saved passwords in Samsung Wallet under Samsung Pass [VERIFY].

Opera / Brave / Vivaldi

These Chromium-based browsers support saved passwords and credential storage. Because they are built on Chromium, the save prompt looks and behaves much like Chrome's. The prompt appears right under the sign-in form.

  • Look for the save prompt when you log in to a site.
  • Open the browser's password settings to view or add saved entries [VERIFY menu names].

Important notes

  • The save prompt may not appear on every site — that is normal.
  • Entries are filed under the page's origin, not under the target site the password is for.
  • Internal chrome:// and edge:// URLs cannot be opened from a web page — use the Copy address buttons below.
  • Browsers never report whether the user saved a password.
    • `n
  • Saving passwords in a shared or public browser is not recommended — use a private profile or sign out when you are done.
  • A dedicated password manager can do more, such as sharing passwords securely and working across every browser and device.
