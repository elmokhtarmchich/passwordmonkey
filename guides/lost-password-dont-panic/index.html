Lost Your Password? Don't Panic

Last updated: October 2026

Losing access to an account is stressful, but there's almost always a way back. Most sites have recovery options, and password managers have safety nets. Here's what to do, step by step, for each situation — calm, practical guidance to help you regain access without making things worse.

Forgot one site password

If you just forgot the password for a single account, follow these steps in order:

  1. Use the site's reset flow: Look for "Forgot password" or "Reset password" on the sign-in page. Most sites will send a reset link or code to the email on file.
  2. Check your password manager first: Before resetting, search for the site in your password manager. You might have saved it and just need to sign in there.
  3. Generate a new, strong password: Never reuse an old password when resetting. Use a unique, random password — you can create one with the PasswordMonkey generator.
  4. Store it safely: Save the new password in your password manager right away so you don't lose it again.

Lost your device

If the device you normally use is lost or stolen, you can still recover access if you're prepared:

  1. Use another device: Sign in to your password manager from a different phone, tablet, or computer you have access to.
  2. Sign in with your master password: Use your password manager's master password to unlock your vault and access all saved logins.
  3. Use recovery options: If you can't sign in on the new device, use your recovery codes, emergency kit, or emergency access as set up in advance.
  4. Remove the lost device: If possible, sign out of or remove the lost device from your password manager's trusted devices list and any accounts where it's still logged in.

Forgot your password manager's master password

For most password managers, the master password cannot be reset. This is by design — it's what encrypts your vault.

  • It may be unrecoverable: If you have no recovery method set up, there is often no way for the provider to restore your vault. That's the point of strong encryption.
  • Bitwarden: There is no master password reset. Premium users can set up Emergency Access to allow a trusted contact to request access with a waiting period.
  • 1Password: Keep your Emergency Kit (which includes your Secret Key) in a safe place. Recovery codes can also help depending on your setup.
  • Apple iCloud Keychain: Access depends on a trusted device passcode, recovery contact, or recovery key. Without those, recovery may not be possible.
  • Prepare now: Before you need it, set up recovery options and store your recovery information safely. There is no universal "forgot master password" reset.

Your password was found in a breach

If you learn a password has been exposed in a data breach, act quickly and precisely:

  1. Change the breached password first: Update it on the affected site immediately with a new, unique, random password.
  2. Check for reuse: Change any other account where you used the same password. Attackers will try reused passwords across sites.
  3. Change on evidence of compromise: Follow NIST guidance — change passwords when there's evidence of compromise, not on an arbitrary fixed schedule.
  4. Go unique going forward: Use unique, random passwords for every account and store them in a password manager.

Be prepared

A little preparation now saves a lot of stress later:

  • Print a Recovery Kit: Use our PasswordMonkey Recovery Kit tool to generate a blank kit, then print it and fill it in by hand.
  • Only record locations: Never store actual passwords in your printed Recovery Kit. Only record where to find recovery information (emergency contacts, kit locations, recovery codes storage).
  • Store it safely: Keep your printed kit in a secure, known location — think a locked drawer or safe, not taped to your monitor.
  • Review regularly: Update your kit when you change devices, switch password managers, or update recovery contacts.

Sources

  • NIST SP 800-63B-4 (final, August 2025) — change passwords on evidence of compromise, not on a fixed schedule.
