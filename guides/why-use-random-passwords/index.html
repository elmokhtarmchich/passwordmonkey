Why Use Random Passwords

Last updated: October 2026

Reused passwords are the biggest risk to your online accounts. If one site is breached, every account using that password is at risk. Random passwords break that chain — when each account has a unique, randomly generated password, a breach at one site cannot cascade to your other accounts.

The three fears people have

What if it gets hacked?

One strong stored password is safer than many weak reused ones. A breach of one account doesn't cascade when passwords are unique. Your password manager encrypts your vault, so your data stays protected even if the service is attacked.

What if I lose access?

Recovery options exist: password reset flows, recovery codes, emergency kits, and trusted contacts. Losing access is stressful, but there's almost always a way back. Keep recovery codes somewhere safe and separate from your manager.

One strong stored password vs reused weak ones

  • Reusing one password across sites means one breach equals all accounts compromised.
  • A random password stored in a manager means you only need to protect one thing: your manager.
  • Length matters most: 15 characters minimum, 16 or more recommended.

Do you need symbols?

No. Length and uniqueness matter most; symbols are optional. NIST SP 800-63B-4 removed composition rules. A long random password or passphrase works fine without symbols. If a site requires symbols, add them — but don't rely on them for strength.

Random password or passphrase?

Both work. A long random password and a passphrase of several random words are both strong. Store either in a password manager so you only need to remember one master password.

How long should my password be?

Use 15 characters or more; 16 or more is recommended. NIST SP 800-63B-4 sets a 15-character minimum for single-factor passwords and allows at least 64. Longer passwords are exponentially harder to crack. If a site limits length, use the maximum it allows and a unique password for that site.

Entropy explained simply

Password strength is measured in entropy (bits). Weak: under 32 bits. Fair: 32-64. Good: 64-128. Strong: 128+. Higher entropy means harder to crack. Aim for 128+ bits for your most important accounts.

Common myths about passwords

  • Myth: "I need to change my password every 90 days." Fact: Change only on evidence of compromise, not on a schedule.
  • Myth: "Symbols make my password strong." Fact: Length and uniqueness matter most.
  • Myth: "I can remember all my passwords." Fact: Use a password manager.
