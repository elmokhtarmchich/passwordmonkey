Secure Your First 5 Accounts

Last updated: October 2026

Don't change every password at once. Start with these five, in order. Change each as you next log in — not all at once. This ladder approach makes security manageable. You build momentum one login at a time, and each step protects the ones that follow.

The ladder

Work through these accounts in order. Each one is a gateway to the rest, so protecting the top of the ladder first keeps everything below it safe.

  1. Email — it resets every other account, so protect it first. Your email is the gateway. Most sites send reset links to your email, so if someone controls your email, they control everything. Use a long, unique password and turn on two-step verification.
  2. Bank — your money. Protect your financial accounts with strong, unique passwords. A bank breach can drain savings fast, so this is your second priority after email.
  3. Phone carrier — SMS-based two-step verification goes to your number. Protect your carrier account with a PIN and port protection. If someone ports your number, they can intercept your text codes.
  4. Main cloud account — device backups and sync. Your cloud account holds photos, documents, and device backups. Lock it down so a thief cannot reach your files or restore your phone.
  5. Main social account — your digital identity. Your social accounts connect to many other services. Securing them stops impersonation and blocks a path into linked apps.

Start at the top. Each account protects the ones below it. Work down the list one at a time, changing a password only when you next log in.

Change as you go

  • Change each password when you next log in to that account
  • Use 15+ characters (16 or more recommended)
  • Store each in your password manager
  • Don't try to do all five in one day — one per login is fine
  • Let your password manager generate the new password so it is random and unique

Two-step verification and passkeys

  • Enable two-step verification on every account that offers it
  • Two-step verification adds a second check beyond your password — an app code or security key
  • Even if your password is stolen, the second step protects your account
  • Passkeys can replace passwords where supported — they're phishing-resistant [NEEDS SOURCE for platform support]
  • Passwords still work everywhere; use passkeys where available and strong random passwords elsewhere
  • Prefer an authenticator app or security key over SMS when you can

On-device checklist

This checklist stores only tick states in localStorage under the key "pm-secure-5-checklist". It never stores any passwords or secrets.

  • Email password changed (15+ chars, stored in manager)
  • Bank password changed (15+ chars, stored in manager)
  • Phone carrier account secured (PIN, port protection)
  • Main cloud account secured (recovery contact/key set up)
  • Main social account secured (two-step verification enabled)
  • Two-step verification enabled on all 5 accounts
  • Recovery kit printed and stored safely

Why this order

  • Your email is the gateway — most sites send reset links to your email, so if someone controls your email, they control everything
  • Your bank protects your money
  • Your phone carrier controls your phone number, which may receive SMS codes
  • Your cloud account holds device backups and sync
  • Your social account is your digital identity
