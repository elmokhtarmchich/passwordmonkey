Where to Save Your Passwords

Last updated: October 2026

Your passwords need a safe home. Writing them down in the wrong place — or reusing them because you can't remember them — puts your accounts at risk. This guide compares your options: dedicated password managers, browser-built-in managers, paper, and what to avoid.

Password manager (recommended)

A password manager is an app that stores your passwords in an encrypted vault. One master password unlocks everything. It generates, saves, and autofills passwords for every site. It works across devices and platforms. Best for: most people with multiple accounts.

Choose a trusted manager with a strong master password. The master password is the only one you need to remember.

Browser-built-in password manager

The manager built into your browser is convenient and already on your device. It stores encrypted credentials on your device. One caveat: entries are filed under the page's origin (the site you're on when you save), not the target site. Best for: people who use one browser and don't need cross-platform access.

For many people this is a fine starting point. Review saved entries often.

Paper

Paper works for a few critical passwords (email, bank, password manager master). Keep it in a locked drawer or safe. No digital attacks are possible, but there is no backup if it is lost or damaged. Best for: a small number of your most important credentials.

Use paper as a backup, not your main method.

What NOT to use

Notes apps are not designed to encrypt secrets. Spreadsheets have no encryption and are easy to copy or forward. Email to yourself is risky because inboxes get breached. Screenshots have no encryption and are often backed up to the cloud. Avoid all of these.

If you already use one, move those passwords to a safer option.

Quick comparison

Option Best for Encryption Cross-device
Dedicated password manager Most people Yes Yes
Browser-built-in Single browser users Yes Limited
Paper Few critical passwords No No
Notes apps / spreadsheets / email Never No No

How to choose

If you have many accounts, use a dedicated password manager. If you use one browser primarily, the browser-built-in is fine. For your most critical passwords (email, bank), consider a paper backup too. Never reuse passwords, regardless of where you save them.

Start with one secure place and move all your passwords there.
